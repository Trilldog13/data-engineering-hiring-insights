# data-engineering-hiring-insights
A 37-day review of a Mid-Level Data Engineering hiring funnel. This case study explores where technical filtering broke down, why take-home failure rates were high, and how restructuring the interview stages improved alignment, efficiency, and candidate quality.

# Mid-Level Data Engineering Hiring Funnel – 37 Day Review

## Data & Confidentiality Note

All information shared in this case study is anonymised and presented at an aggregated level. No candidate data, proprietary systems, or sensitive company information from Ocado Technology has been disclosed. The purpose of this write-up is to share process learnings and hiring optimisation insights only.

---

## Overview 

This example highlights a 37-day hiring cycle for a Mid-Level Data Engineer role, which surfaced structural challenges in our interview process.

After reviewing the data, it became clear that we were progressing too many candidates into the interview stages who did not meet the required technical bar. This was particularly evident at the Take-Home stage, where we saw a high failure rate.

## Key Learning

One of the biggest learnings from this process was that technical depth is far harder to assess conversationally than practically. Live discussion can often overestimate applied capability.

Our goal was never to reduce pass rates — it was to ensure better alignment between role expectations and candidate readiness, while respecting everyone’s time.

Although moving the technical assessment earlier meant candidates didn’t initially speak to the hiring team before submitting their work, it ultimately created a fairer and more objective evaluation of practical ability.

---

## What We Should Have Clarified Better

For this role, “mid-level” meant:

- Independent ownership and delivery of data pipelines  
- Confidence in SQL optimisation  
- Practical understanding of dimensional modelling (Kimball principles)  
- Applied experience using tools such as DBT  

It wasn’t just familiarity with concepts it required demonstrable, hands-on capability.

Greater clarity around this expectation earlier in the process would likely have improved alignment and reduced unnecessary progression through the funnel.

---

## What We Observed

A significant proportion of candidates struggled with the technical take-home challenge. This indicated that our initial screening stage (Hiring Manager interview) was not filtering strongly enough for hands-on technical capability.

As a result, we were:

- Investing time in candidates who were not technically ready  
- Creating unnecessary workload for reviewers  
- Slowing overall process efficiency  

---

## The Technical Take-Home Challenge

The take-home assessment was designed to reflect real-world mid-level expectations and focused on:

- ~40 minutes of Python-based data manipulation  
- SQL problem solving  
- Building and reasoning about data pipelines  
- DBT fundamentals  
- Data warehouse concepts, particularly Kimball modelling principles  

The challenge was intentionally practical. It assessed applied engineering ability rather than theoretical knowledge.

---

## Process Improvement

To address the issue, we made a structural change:

We swapped Stages 1 and 2 moving the technical take-home earlier in the funnel and positioning the Hiring Manager conversation after technical validation.

This ensured:

- Stronger technical filtering earlier  
- More meaningful Hiring Manager conversations  
- A more efficient funnel  
- Improved candidate quality in later stages  

Moving the technical assessment earlier allowed candidates to demonstrate capability upfront and ensured that later interviews focused on depth, judgement, and collaboration rather than baseline validation.
